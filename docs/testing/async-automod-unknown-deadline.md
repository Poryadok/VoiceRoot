1. DISPOSITION: IMPLEMENT THE PRODUCER; DO NOT CLAIM AN IMPOSSIBLE FAILURE GUARANTEE
The current missing protected lifecycle producer is a real implementation gap. Implement the Auth transactional lifecycle journal/outbox, exact consumer projection, positive terminal authority and complete catch-up described below. Those are useful active-path work, not a disabled foundation.

The stronger statement 'physically erase this online participant at an Auth-only deadline despite a total partition that hides the deletion, while never erasing an indistinguishable live account' is impossible for the current database-per-service ownership model. It is not repaired by another retry loop, a timestamp, a boot census or a larger retention TTL. c18 cannot be marked privacy-complete for that failure scenario merely by implementing the producer.

Concrete indistinguishability proof: at time C Mod has a LIVE subject with valid retained strike/alias data. World L: Auth stays LIVE. World D: Auth commits deletion at t>C and its canonical deadline is D=t+P30D. Both lifecycle delivery and every authoritative lookup are partitioned until after D. Mod receives exactly the same inputs in both worlds. Erasing the account in D also erases the live account in L; retaining it in L also retains the deleted account in D. Random choice, deny-all readiness and local clocks add no missing identity/deletion information. Thirty-day logical strike expiry does not erase sixty-day physical aliases, raw mapping or effective sanctions.

There is a symmetric case: Mod has observed DELETE(cycle X, deadline D), but Auth validly restores before D and the newer LIVE revision is hidden by the partition. An old scheduled deadline alone cannot prove that X remained the winning lifecycle. Blind local terminalization can destroy eligible live-account history. This is a correction to the overly broad c9 'known deadline is sufficient even with Auth unavailable' oracle, not a new strike rule. Preserve automatic destructive work for positively terminal authority; fail closed on unresolved lifecycle rather than inventing terminality.

Selected engineering boundary: Auth owns lifecycle decisions and permanent terminalization; recipients own physical cleanup. A genuine at-deadline physical-erasure claim requires the winning owner decision already settled at the recipient and executing local cleanup by that deadline. The current restore-until-deadline model cannot establish this in every execution; even a last-moment valid restore with finite transport latency exposes the distinction. No distributed zero-time completion or new cleanup grace is asserted. Application/public-route outages can be tolerated through independent protected private listeners/outbox/pull recovery and local cleanup, but a total loss of every authority path or an unavailable storage engine cannot be claimed to satisfy physical erasure. A missed physical deadline is a privacy-operational failure, not an ACK-based deadline extension or an allowed longer retention policy. The original deadline remains recorded; cleanup on recovery is late cleanup.

The required unconditional arbitrary-partition physical guarantee is therefore a precise remaining acceptance blocker in the current architecture. No test can establish it for this architecture. Resolving that stronger requirement would require an explicit different storage/decision/failure model, not an ordinary consumer fix. This decision does not introduce shared databases, a cross-service transaction, key escrow or crypto-erasure as an undisclosed substitute. No routine user question is needed to implement the concrete safe scope below.
